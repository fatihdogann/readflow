A Pocket alternative that keeps the archive on your computer.

Pocket was shut down in 2025. Readflow collects articles, links and PDFs in one place, builds your reading list and keeps highlights on your own device. Summaries come from the AI tool already on your computer — no API key.

See setup Source on GitHub

Open source · MIT · Pocket/Instapaper/Readwise import · Offline reading

Why Readflow?

What you want from a reading list: easy to save, easy to find, and your data stays yours.

Take over your Pocket archive

Import links, titles and tags from a Pocket, Instapaper or Readwise CSV/HTML export you saved earlier. Article content is fetched again from the links; old highlights and notes are not imported.

Local-first

The archive, highlights and summaries live on your computer. No cloud account, no service lock-in.

PDFs and scanned documents

PDFs are archived alongside web articles, and scanned pages are turned into readable text with local OCR.

Summaries with your own AI tool

Short/Normal/Detailed summaries and readability edits with a CLI on your machine, such as Claude or Codex. Every output keeps its version history.

Offline and fast

The last 60 pages you opened are cached in the browser, readable on a plane, on the metro, or while your Mac sleeps.

Export freedom

Export to Markdown, TXT, DOCX or PDF, and optionally connect Notion and Telegram.

Quick setup

Runs locally and starts with a few commands. Full steps are on the home page.

git clone https://github.com/fatihdogann/readflow
cd readflow
pnpm install
pnpm build
# a claude / codex / jcode CLI session must be signed in
pnpm app:start      # http://127.0.0.1:3000

Frequently asked

Did Pocket shut down?

Yes, Pocket was shut down in 2025, so saved articles need an alternative reading archive.

How do I move my archive?

Import a CSV/HTML export you saved earlier: links, titles and tags are added, and articles are fetched again. Old highlights and notes are not imported.

Is it free?

Yes. Open source (MIT) and no API key; your own AI tool produces the summaries.

Do I need internet?

Yes to save, no to read: the last 60 opened pages are readable offline.
